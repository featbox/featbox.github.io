[01] The approach

Method before tools.

AI without a process is automated chaos. Featbox starts from three principles, and the tools exist to keep them.

01

One role for each AI

Each assistant does what it does best: planning, research, review. The project context is the same for all of them.

02

Humans decide

The AI proposes, researches and executes. What becomes a rule goes through a person's approval and is recorded with date and source.

03

You choose what to share

Your project stays in files on your computer. When working with an assistant, you choose which information to share.

[02] The tools

Three tools, one way of working.

Start with reading, organize your work and track decisions. These are captures of the apps in development (0.5.3), using fictional data.

FB/010.5.3
The reader

FeatReader

Read documents, highlight passages and save notes and audio in each document’s notebook. Opens md, txt, pdf and docx, with text-to-speech on your computer. Free.

FB/020.5.3
The workbench

FeatDesk

Write and find your notes in a folder on your computer. Organize work into Research, Work and Export. Your files remain yours and open in other editors.

FB/030.5.3
The decision room

FeatRoom

Record proposals, approvals and rejections so you can revisit the reasons behind each decision. Browse your personal or project history and check record integrity.

[05] In practice

What we can already show.

See what the demo screens show and what you will be able to evaluate during the pilot.

demonstrated

A folder you own as the starting point.

Documents, notes and records stay on your computer. Version 0.5.3 is presented for local use; anything you share with other assistants depends on your choices in those services.

demonstrated

Only what you approve becomes a rule.

The AI logs a decision request; you approve or reject it. Each event type has its own color, and a rejection is recorded just like an approval.

demonstrated

A history you can check.

The app checks the links between records and reports inconsistencies. This helps identify changes; it does not prevent file deletion or replace a backup.

demonstrated

The why stays next to the code.

Each module of the project has a short card describing what it does. When new code lands after it, the badge changes from "up to date" to "outdated", so you know what the AI should no longer trust.

in development

Understand the cost of revisiting context.

Long conversations can require an assistant to re-read a lot of information. A usage meter is being tested to make that consumption more visible as you work.

feature in testing · savings not yet demonstrated
in evaluation

Your work stays accessible.

Notes are open-format files in a folder you own. You can open them in other editors. The pilot will help evaluate installation and everyday use in real projects.

What to check before trying it
  • Compatibility: Windows 10 or 11. Version 0.5.3 in evaluation; per-user installation.
  • Files: Markdown notes and documents in your own folders. Keep backups as you would for any project.
  • Decisions: the history records proposals, approvals and rejections. Integrity checks report inconsistencies between records.
  • Sharing: when using other assistants, check what you send and each service’s terms.
  • In development: local memory, project questions and usage tracking. These are not presented as finished features.
